# Musio Market Research
## March 2026 refresh

Last updated: March 29, 2026

---

## Bottom line

The market is moving in two directions at once:

1. **AI music capability keeps improving fast** — longer outputs, better structure control, more mainstream distribution.
2. **Trust, rights, and discovery are getting messier** — spam, impersonation, and policy backlash are all intensifying.

That combination is important for Musio.

If Musio wants to grow in a way that can eventually sell well, the strongest lane is **not** "be another text-to-song app." It is more likely **trusted music workflow infrastructure**: realistic musical output, tighter control, cleaner rights story, and genuinely useful creator workflow.

## Verified facts

### 1) The music market is still large and growing
- **IFPI** reported global recorded music revenue reached **$31.7B in 2025**, up **6.4% YoY**.
- Paid subscription streaming grew **8.8%**.
- Paid streaming subscription accounts reached **837M** globally.
- Source: IFPI, March 18, 2026  
  <https://www.ifpi.org/global-music-report-2026-global-recorded-music-revenues-grow-6-4-as-record-companies-drive-innovation/>

### 2) Google is pushing AI music toward longer, more structured creation
- **Google** said **Lyria 3 Pro** can generate tracks up to **3 minutes** long.
- Google also said users can prompt for **intros, verses, choruses, and bridges**.
- Google embedded Lyria music generation into **Gemini** and **Google Vids**.
- Sources: Google Workspace Updates, March 25, 2026  
  <https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/03/create-longer-musical-tracks-in-gemini-app-with-Lyria-3-Pro.html>  
  <https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/03/generate-custom-music-in-google-vids-powered-by-Lyria-3-and-Lyria-3-Pro.html?hl=en>

### 3) Real creator usage still skews toward assistive tools, not full-song generation
- **Moises x Water & Music** surveyed **1,525 musicians**.
- **67%** of respondents said they had used AI for music-related work.
- **78% of pros** reported AI use vs **60% of hobbyists**.
- Among AI users, **stem separation** led at **71%** adoption.
- **Full-song generation** was only **24%**.
- **58%** worried about authenticity; **55%** worried about copyright and licensing.
- Source: Moises / Water & Music, March 2026  
  <https://moises.ai/insights/musician-ai-report-water-and-music/>

### 4) Copyright/policy momentum is not clearly moving toward free-for-all training
- **BBC** reported the UK government backed away from its previously favored opt-out approach for AI training on copyrighted works.
- The government said it **"no longer has a preferred option."**
- That does **not** settle the issue, but it is a real setback for broad uncompensated training proposals.
- Source: BBC, March 18, 2026  
  <https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cvg1gr5v333o>

### 5) Oversupply and impersonation remain serious distribution problems
- **TIME** reported **Deezer** said **50,000 AI-generated tracks per day** were being uploaded and accounted for **34% of new uploads**.
- TIME also reported **Sony** had sought removal of **135,000+** tracks impersonating its artists.
- Source: TIME, March 26, 2026  
  <https://time.com/7277468/ai-music-streaming-deezer-spotify/>

### 6) UNESCO is warning of meaningful creator-income pressure
- **UNESCO** said generative AI outputs are projected to cause **24% global revenue losses for music creators by 2028**.
- UNESCO also highlighted **platform concentration** and **opaque curation systems** as structural problems.
- Source: UNESCO, March 27, 2026  
  <https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/creators-face-projected-global-revenue-losses-24-2028-new-unesco-report-shows>

### 7) Competitors are moving toward deeper workflow, not just prompt boxes
- **Suno**'s **MILO-1080** lab points toward sequencer-like workflow and producer-oriented creation.
- Public details on the page are limited, so this point should be treated as directional rather than fully documented.
- Source: Suno  
  <https://suno.com/labs/milo>

## Interpretation

### What seems true now

#### A) The market is bifurcating
There is a huge difference between:
- **consumer novelty / content volume**, and
- **serious creator workflow / commercially usable output**.

The second category looks more durable.

#### B) Trust is becoming part of product value
Because the market is getting flooded with AI uploads, deepfakes, and rights disputes, products that feel:
- clean,
- controllable,
- realistic,
- commercially safe

should become more valuable relative to generic generators.

#### C) Full-song generation is visible, but assistive workflow still looks more real
Google and Suno are making generation better. That matters.

But the best evidence in hand says musicians are still using AI most for **specific utility tasks** inside a workflow, especially stem separation and adjacent assistance, not mainly for one-click finished songs.

#### D) Rights posture is increasingly strategic, not just legal hygiene
A clean licensing story can help with:
- enterprise partnerships,
- sync/commercial use,
- education,
- acquisition attractiveness,
- user trust.

## Where Musio fits

### Strongest positioning
Musio appears better positioned as:
- **a creator-first music workflow tool**, not a pure generative toy;
- **realistic musical output infrastructure**;
- **a trustworthy alternative** to sloppier AI music categories;
- **a controllable bridge** between musical intent and usable output.

### Weakest positioning
Musio is less advantaged if it tries to compete directly as:
- a generic prompt-to-song app,
- a mass-volume consumer AI music novelty product,
- an all-things-to-all-creators platform with no specific wedge.

---

## If the goal is to grow to sell

### What probably increases sellability
- Strong retention with serious creators
- Clear commercial-use / rights story
- Distinctive realism or human-feel quality that generic generators lack
- Workflow integrations that make Musio part of the stack
- Proof that users come for output quality and stay for efficiency/control

### Likely buyer logic
Potential acquirers would probably care about whether Musio is:
- a differentiated **workflow layer**,
- a differentiated **sound / realism layer**,
- a differentiated **rights-safe creator layer**,
- or a niche with unusually strong retention and trust.

### What would weaken sellability
- Looking interchangeable with bigger AI music platforms
- Weak differentiation vs Google/Suno/Udio
- Any ambiguity around rights or commercial usage
- Shallow curiosity usage instead of repeat workflow usage

---

## Practical implication for Musio messaging

Best message direction:

**Real musicality. Human control. Useful output. Trustworthy commercial posture.**

That feels better aligned with where the market is actually going than leaning too hard into generic AI-song hype.
