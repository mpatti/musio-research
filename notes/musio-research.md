# Musio Market Research
## March 2026 refresh

Last updated: March 29, 2026 (morning)

---

## Bottom line

Two things look true at once:

1. **AI music is becoming a real scaled market** — Google is distributing Lyria deeper into mainstream products, and Suno now claims meaningful paid scale.
2. **Trust, rights, and discovery are getting messier** — policy pressure, spam, impersonation, and creator skepticism all remain real.

That combination still points Musio away from the generic prompt-to-song race and toward **trusted creator workflow infrastructure**.

---

## Verified facts

### 1) Google is pushing AI music deeper into mainstream workflow software
- **Google** said **Lyria 3 Pro** can generate tracks up to **3 minutes** long.
- It also said users can prompt for **intros, verses, choruses, and bridges**.
- Google embedded music generation into **Google Vids**, not just Gemini.
- Sources: Google Workspace Updates, March 25, 2026  
  <https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/03/create-longer-musical-tracks-in-gemini-app-with-Lyria-3-Pro.html>  
  <https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/03/generate-custom-music-in-google-vids-powered-by-Lyria-3-and-Lyria-3-Pro.html?hl=en>

### 2) Suno is claiming serious paid scale
- **Music Business Worldwide** reported Suno CEO **Mikey Shulman** said Suno reached:
  - **2M paid subscribers**
  - **$300M ARR**
  - **100M users total**
- This is a **company claim**, not an audited filing, so it should be treated as important but not fully verified.
- Source: Music Business Worldwide, Feb. 25, 2026  
  <https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/suno-hits-2m-paid-subscribers-300m-annual-revenue/>

### 3) The recorded-music market remains large and growing
- **IFPI** reported global recorded music revenue reached **$31.7B in 2025**, up **6.4% YoY**.
- Paid subscription streaming grew **8.8%**.
- Paid streaming subscription accounts reached **837M** globally.
- Source: IFPI, March 18, 2026  
  <https://www.ifpi.org/global-music-report-2026-global-recorded-music-revenues-grow-6-4-as-record-companies-drive-innovation/>

### 4) Real creator usage still skews toward assistive tools, not full-song generation
- **Moises x Water & Music** surveyed **1,525 musicians**.
- **67%** said they had used AI for music-related work.
- **78% of pros** reported AI use vs **60% of hobbyists**.
- Among AI users, **stem separation** led at **71%** adoption.
- **Full-song generation** was only **24%**.
- **58%** worried about authenticity and **55%** worried about copyright/licensing.
- Source: Moises / Water & Music, March 2026  
  <https://moises.ai/insights/musician-ai-report-water-and-music/>

### 5) Copyright/policy momentum is still contested, not settled in AI’s favor
- **BBC** reported the UK government backed away from its previously favored opt-out approach for AI training on copyrighted works.
- It said it **"no longer has a preferred option."**
- That is not a final policy win for rightsholders, but it is a real setback for broad uncompensated-training proposals.
- Source: BBC, March 18, 2026  
  <https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cvg1gr5v333o>

### 6) Oversupply and impersonation remain major streaming-layer problems
- **TIME** reported **Deezer** said **50,000 AI-generated tracks/day** were being uploaded and represented **34% of new uploads**.
- TIME also reported **Sony** had sought removal of **135,000+** tracks impersonating its artists.
- Source: TIME, March 26, 2026  
  <https://time.com/7277468/ai-music-streaming-deezer-spotify/>

### 7) UNESCO is warning of meaningful creator-income pressure
- **UNESCO** said generative AI outputs are projected to cause **24% global revenue losses for music creators by 2028**.
- It also highlighted **platform concentration** and **opaque curation systems** as structural problems.
- Source: UNESCO, March 27, 2026  
  <https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/creators-face-projected-global-revenue-losses-24-2028-new-unesco-report-shows>

## Interpretation

### What seems true now

#### A) The category is commercially real
Google’s distribution and Suno’s claimed paid scale both suggest AI music is not just hype anymore.

#### B) But broad category success does **not** mean Musio should copy the leaders
Those same signals also mean the generic AI-music-app lane is getting more crowded and harder to win.

#### C) Workflow utility still looks more durable than one-click finished songs
The strongest usage evidence still favors tools that help musicians inside real workflows.

#### D) Trust is becoming part of product value
As spam, impersonation, and rights disputes rise, products that feel:
- clean,
- controllable,
- realistic,
- commercially safe

should gain strategic value.

---

## Where Musio fits

### Strongest positioning
Musio appears better positioned as:
- **creator-first workflow infrastructure**,
- **realistic musical output infrastructure**,
- **a controllable bridge** between musical intent and usable output,
- **a safer commercial/trust story** than sloppier AI-music categories.

### Weakest positioning
Musio is less advantaged if it tries to compete directly as:
- a generic prompt-to-song app,
- a mass-volume consumer novelty product,
- a platform with no specific trust/workflow wedge.

---

## If the goal is to grow to sell

### What probably increases sellability
- Strong retention with serious creators
- Clear commercial-use / rights story
- Distinctive realism or human-feel quality
- Workflow integrations that make Musio part of the stack
- Proof that users stay for efficiency/control, not curiosity

### What weakens sellability
- Looking interchangeable with Google/Suno/Udio-style products
- Weak differentiation versus larger AI music platforms
- Any ambiguity around rights or commercial usage

---

## Practical implication for Musio messaging

Best message direction:

**Real musicality. Human control. Useful output. Trustworthy commercial posture.**
