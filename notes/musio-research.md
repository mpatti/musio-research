## Bottom line

The evening check did **not** produce a category reversal.

The strongest useful new signals are:
- the **UK government backed away from an AI-training opt-out path** after creative-industry backlash,
- and **Google is now part of the music-training litigation fight** via a new Lyria 3 lawsuit from indie creators.

That reinforces the same core conclusion from earlier today: the market keeps getting more explicit about **rights, provenance, impersonation, and trust**.

## Verified facts

### 1) Google is still pushing AI music into mainstream workflow surfaces
- **Google** said **Lyria 3 Pro** can generate tracks up to **3 minutes** long.
- It also said users can prompt for **intros, verses, choruses, and bridges**.
- Google said the feature is rolling into the **Gemini app** and broader workflow surfaces.
- Source: Google Workspace Updates, March 25, 2026  
  <https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/03/create-longer-musical-tracks-in-gemini-app-with-Lyria-3-Pro.html>

### 2) Google has now been pulled into music-training litigation too
- **Billboard** reported a proposed class action by indie artists, songwriters, and producers alleging **Google trained Lyria 3 on unlicensed YouTube songs**.
- The plaintiffs argue Google used its control of YouTube to move from distributor to competitor.
- **Google's previously reported position** is that Lyria trains only on music Google and YouTube have a right to use under terms, partner agreements, and applicable law.
- This is an **allegation**, not a legal finding.
- Source: Billboard, March 9, 2026  
  <https://www.billboard.com/pro/google-ai-music-lawsuit-artists-lyria-3-model-youtube-songs/>

### 3) Suno is still the clearest paid-scale signal in AI music
- **Music Business Worldwide** previously reported Suno CEO **Mikey Shulman** said Suno reached:
  - **2M paid subscribers**
  - **$300M ARR**
  - **100M users total**
- This remains a **company claim**, not an audited filing.
- Source: Music Business Worldwide, Feb. 25, 2026  
  <https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/suno-hits-2m-paid-subscribers-300m-annual-revenue/>

### 4) Suno is also moving deeper into creator personalization
- **Music Business Worldwide** reported Suno launched **v5.5** with:
  - **voice capture**,
  - **private-by-default voice verification**,
  - and **custom models** tuned on a user's own catalog.
- That is important because it suggests the category is moving beyond simple prompts toward creator-specific workflow and identity layers.
- It also increases the importance of **consent, voice controls, and provenance**.
- Source: Music Business Worldwide, March 27, 2026  
  <https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/suno-launches-v5-5-ai-model-with-voice-capture-and-personalization-features/>

### 5) The recorded-music market remains large and growing, and fraud is now explicitly central
- **IFPI** reported global recorded music revenue reached **$31.7B in 2025**, up **6.4% YoY**.
- **Reuters** reported streaming now accounts for around **70%** of recorded music income and total streaming revenue surpassed **$22B**.
- Paid streaming subscription accounts reached **837M** globally.
- IFPI also explicitly called streaming fraud **"theft, plain and simple."**
- Sources: IFPI + Reuters, March 18, 2026  
  <https://www.ifpi.org/global-music-report-2026-global-recorded-music-revenues-grow-6-4-as-record-companies-drive-innovation/>  
  <https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/streaming-boosts-global-music-revenues-once-again-2025-report-shows-2026-03-18/>

### 6) Music-specific copyright pressure is widening, not narrowing
- **Reuters Legal** reported **BMG** sued Anthropic over alleged use of copyrighted lyrics in model training.
- Reuters said BMG cited **493 examples** of alleged infringement and criticized sourcing from **unauthorized torrent sites**.
- Reuters also reported **UMG, Concord, and ABKCO** pressed the existing Anthropic lyrics case by arguing Claude can reproduce lyrics on demand and substitute for the originals.
- Sources: Reuters Legal, March 18 and March 24, 2026  
  <https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/bmg-sues-anthropic-using-bruno-mars-rolling-stones-lyrics-ai-training-2026-03-18/>  
  <https://www.reuters.com/legal/legalindustry/us-music-publishers-suing-anthropic-make-their-case-against-ai-fair-use-2026-03-24/>

### 7) The UK backed away from a creator-hostile default on AI training
- **Billboard** reported the **UK government** said it no longer had a preferred option for an AI-training approach that would have required rightsholders to opt out.
- This does **not** settle the policy debate, but it is a meaningful sign that creator backlash can still change government direction.
- Source: Billboard, March 23, 2026  
  <https://www.billboard.com/pro/uk-music-industry-wins-government-abandons-ai-opt-out-plan/>

### 8) Europe still has a live Suno test around output similarity
- **Music Business Worldwide** reported the Munich court heard **GEMA's** case against **Suno** on March 9.
- GEMA alleges Suno outputs are **"misleadingly similar"** to protected songs in melody, harmony, and rhythm.
- The hearing ended **without a ruling**, and a decision date was set for **June 12, 2026**.
- Source: Music Business Worldwide, March 9, 2026  
  <https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/gema-vs-suno-german-court-hears-landmark-ai-music-copyright-case/>

### 9) Sweden's STIM still matters because it shows licensing paths can exist
- **Reuters** reported **STIM** introduced a licence allowing AI companies to legally use copyrighted songs for training while paying royalties to creators.
- The licence also includes tracking requirements for AI-generated outputs.
- This is **not** yet a broad market standard, but it is one of the clearest examples that music AI can move toward licensing infrastructure instead of only lawsuits.
- Source: Reuters, Sept. 9, 2025  
  <https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/sweden-launches-ai-music-licence-protect-songwriters-2025-09-09/>

### 10) Real creator usage still skews toward assistive tools, not full-song generation
- **Moises x Water & Music** surveyed **1,525 musicians**.
- **67%** said they had used AI for music-related work.
- **78% of pros** reported AI use vs **60%** of hobbyists.
- Among AI users, **stem separation** led at **71%** adoption.
- **Full-song generation** was only **24%**.
- **58%** worried about authenticity and **55%** worried about copyright/licensing.
- Source: Moises / Water & Music, March 2026  
  <https://moises.ai/insights/musician-ai-report-water-and-music/>

### 11) The distribution layer is dealing with large-scale impersonation and AI spam
- **Music Business Worldwide**, citing **BBC** and Sony executive Dennis Kooker, reported **Sony Music** has targeted **135,000+** AI deepfakes of its artists for removal from streaming platforms.
- **Reuters** reported **Deezer** licensed its AI-detection technology to **Sacem**, flagged **13.4M+** AI tracks, receives about **60,000 fully AI-created tracks per day**, and said up to **85%** of AI-generated streams removed from its royalty pool in 2025 were fraudulent.
- Sources: Music Business Worldwide, March 19, 2026; Reuters, Jan. 29, 2026  
  <https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/sony-music-has-targeted-135000-deepfakes-of-its-artists-music-for-removal-from-streaming-platforms/>  
  <https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/deezer-licenses-ai-detection-tool-french-royalty-agency-sacem-plans-wider-roll-2026-01-29/>

## Interpretation

### What seems true now

#### A) The category is real, but legal/trust pressure is becoming part of the product itself
Google and Suno still show that AI music is commercially real.

But the stronger signal is that **trust, provenance, and rights risk** are now shaping what platforms, rightsholders, courts, and governments care about.

#### B) Workflow utility still looks more durable than one-click finished songs
The best creator-usage evidence still favors tools that remove friction in real workflows.

That supports products that help people make music better or faster, not just products that generate more music.

#### C) Rights posture is becoming part of enterprise value
The market is increasingly drawing distinctions around:
- lawful sourcing,
- consent,
- impersonation risk,
- and whether outputs create substitution / takedown / moderation headaches.

That matters directly if the goal is to build something someone would want to buy.

#### D) Trust infrastructure is becoming part of music-tech itself
Sony takedowns, Deezer detection, the UK policy retreat, and Google litigation all point the same direction:
**catalog cleanliness and artist identity protection are becoming product categories.**

## Where Musio fits

### Strongest positioning
Musio appears better positioned as:
- **creator-first workflow infrastructure**,
- **realistic musical output infrastructure**,
- **a controllable bridge** between musical intent and usable output,
- **a cleaner commercial / trust story** than sloppier AI-music categories.

### Weakest positioning
Musio is less advantaged if it tries to compete directly as:
- a generic prompt-to-song app,
- a mass-volume consumer novelty product,
- a platform with no specific trust/workflow wedge,
- or anything that creates identity / provenance risk without strong controls.

---

## If the goal is to grow to sell

### What probably increases sellability
- Strong retention with serious creators
- Clear commercial-use / rights story
- Distinctive realism or human-feel quality
- Workflow integrations that make Musio part of the stack
- Proof that users stay for efficiency/control, not curiosity
- Evidence that Musio reduces trust / provenance headaches instead of adding them

### What weakens sellability
- Looking interchangeable with Google/Suno/Udio-style products
- Weak differentiation versus larger AI music platforms
- Any ambiguity around training data, output ownership, or commercial usage
- Features that raise voice / identity concerns without obvious consent controls

## Practical implication for Musio messaging

Best message direction:

**Real musicality. Human control. Useful workflow. Trustworthy commercial posture.**
